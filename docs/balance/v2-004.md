# v2-004 — the first measurement of v2.0 as a game

The three snapshots before this one measured a **model**: `sim/v2/bleed.ts`
played v2.0's arithmetic while the engine still ran the v1.3 per-act pool.
This one measures the engine. Every number below comes from `reduce`.

300 runs per character, `--gate3`, calibration unchanged
(`vocabularyGap=0.1125`).

## Where it lands

| | CH.01 Linguist | CH.02 Gambler | CH.03 Cryptographer | §11.5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| start | 14 | 9 | 11 | — |
| win rate | **63.0%** | 46.7% | 40.3% | 20–30% |
| no relics bought | **48.3%** | 34.3% | 18.7% | <2% |
| Act I deaths | 8.7% | 27.3% | 23.0% | 10–20% |
| median relics at death | 4 | — | — | 3–5 |
| median emergency buys | 0 | 0 | 0 | >0 |
| word-luck deaths | 16.2% | 23.1% | 25.1% | <5% |

One target is met outright: **relics held at death is 4**, inside the 3–5 band,
which says the acquisition curve is roughly right even though everything it is
attached to is too generous. Act I deaths are in band for two of three
characters.

Everything else is too easy, and by a lot.

## The three readings that matter

**1 · The starting bankroll is the whole difficulty curve.** 14 → 9 is five
guesses and **16 points of win rate**. Nothing else in the game moves the number
that hard. That is a warning as much as a lever: a run's outcome is being
decided by a constant chosen before the first keystroke, which is the opposite
of what §2.2 wants.

**2 · CH.03 starts higher than CH.02 and wins less** — 11 against 9, and 40.3%
against 46.7%. The Gambler's `<= 3` payout bonus fires on 43% of words and is
worth about 0.43 bankroll each; the Cryptographer's innate trades a guess for a
letter, which is a 2:1 loss at §2.3's rates unless the letter saves two guesses.
Measured, it does not. **The Cryptographer's innate is priced wrong**, and it is
the one character innate the value budget of R-048 was never applied to.

**3 · The model under-read the real engine by 16 points.** `v2-003` measured a
relic-less CH.01 at 32%; the engine says 48.3%. The model was missing three
things the engine has: elite nodes pay 70g against a word's 40g, the Twins pays
**two** payouts for one word's guesses, and a run reaches 12 shops rather than
the model's abstract gold trickle. Each is small; together they are 16 points.
The standing lesson is the one from `v2-001`: **a model of the economy is worth
having and is not evidence.**

## What is not the cause

- **Not the valves.** Mean refills bought is 1.81 of 6, and the median emergency
  purchase is **zero** — the ladder is barely touched. R-042, R-043 and R-046
  did their job; the run is not surviving on bought bankroll.
- **Not relic power.** A relic-less CH.01 wins 48.3% against a full-build 63.0%.
  The board is worth ~15 points, which against R-048's budget is about right.
  The problem is what the *floor* is, not what the ceiling adds.
- **Not the solver.** 3.66 guesses a word in-run, against a calibrated clean
  baseline of 3.87. The gap is the INFO relics doing their job.

## What is

Gold. A run earns roughly 920g and spends 696g, which is exactly §4.2's stated
"about 300g per act" — so income is on spec and the spec is too rich for a
board of five slots at these prices. The bot buys about five relics and still
finishes holding 222g.

And underneath that: **a 20-word run against a 24-cap bankroll has very little
room between "trivial" and "unplayable"**, which is the finding `v2-003` closed
on and which the live engine now confirms from the other direction. At start 14
the game is a formality; at start 9 it is 46.7%, still nearly twice target.
Reaching 20–30% by lowering the start alone would need something near 6, and a
run that starts at 6 is over on word three.

## Recommendation, not yet applied

Three levers, in the order they should be tried, because each one changes what
the next one measures:

1. **Income.** 920g a run against 5 slots at 60/110/180 means the shelf is not a
   choice. Cutting node gold ~30% (40/70/120 → 30/50/90) makes a rare a real
   commitment. Cheapest change, and it is the one §4.2 explicitly guesses at.
2. **The payout base.** 6 is a knife edge — `v2-003` measured base 7 taking a
   relic-less run from 32% to 85%. Base 5 moves break-even to 2.5, which makes
   the median 3.66-guess word cost 2.3 instead of 1.3. This is the biggest
   single lever and the most dangerous.
3. **The Cryptographer's innate**, on its own merits, whatever happens above.

None of the three is applied here. This snapshot is the measurement the
migration owed, and picking between the three is a design call.
