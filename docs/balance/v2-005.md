# v2-005 — rarity gates supply, and the shelf becomes a choice

1000 runs, `npm run sim -- --runs 1000 --gate3 --snapshot v2-005`, CH.01,
calibration unchanged (`vocabularyGap=0.1125`). Reads against `v2-004`; every
comparison below is the same 1000 seeds with only the config changed.

Ruled as MECHANICS.md R-052 and R-053. The reasoning is `ADR-0013`.

## Rarity was decoration

`relics.json` carried a `rarity` on every entry, the card spine coloured itself
from it, and §4.2 looked a price up in it. **No draw consulted it.**
`rollShopStock` weighted by archetype and nothing else, so a tier's share of a
shelf was however many relics happened to be in that tier.

The shelf-eligible registry is **3 COMMON, 10 UNCOMMON, 5 RARE** — §6.4's impact
test cut most of the commons — which unweighted is 16.7 / 55.6 / 27.8. Measured
over 600 seeds per act, that is exactly what was on the shelf:

| | Act I | Act II | Act III | | Act I | Act II | Act III |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | **before** | | | | **after** | | |
| COMMON | 16.6% | 17.7% | 15.9% | | 32.8% | 28.3% | 18.9% |
| UNCOMMON | 59.8% | 59.4% | 56.2% | | 56.8% | 55.1% | 54.2% |
| RARE | **23.7%** | **22.9%** | **27.9%** | | **10.3%** | **16.7%** | **26.8%** |

Two readings of the before column:

1. **The distribution was the pool's shape and nothing else.** A rare appeared
   on about three shelves in four. That is not a rare.
2. **The three columns are one column.** A run in Act III shopped from the shelf
   it shopped from in Act I.

The after column is within 1.5 points of `cfg.rarityWeights` in every cell; the
drift is §6.6's archetype bias, which multiplies on top and is intended.

**The curve is 35/55/10 → 28/55/17 → 20/52/28, not the 60/30/10 → 26/46/28 this
snapshot was first written against.** With only three COMMON relics in the
registry, a 60% Act I share hands 60% of three relic slots to three relics — and
three slots then show the same three relics in every early shop. So UNCOMMON
stays the backbone in all three acts and **RARE is what moves**, a tenth of the
shelf to over a quarter. Twelve shops at three relic slots is 36 draws a run:
about one RARE in Act I, about three in Act III.

## Price was doing rarity's job

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| COMMON | 60g | **40g** |
| UNCOMMON | 110g | **70g** |
| RARE | 180g | **110g** |
| BOSS (sell-back only) | 180g | **150g** |
| CONSUMABLE | 40g | **30g** |
| whole shelf, Act I | 395g | **223g** |
| whole shelf, Act III | 405g | **255g** |
| node gold (word/elite/boss) | 40/70/120 | **30/50/90** |

A price is bad at making something rare. A player who wants the rare saves two
nodes and buys it; a player who cannot afford one is left with a shelf they can
do nothing with. Neither is scarcity, and the second is the failure `v2-004`
named from the other direction — *"920g a run against five slots at 60/110/180
means the shelf is not a choice."*

So supply carries the restriction and the prices come down behind it. **The
income comes down with them**, at `v2-004`'s recommended 30/50/90: cheaper
relics against unchanged income would make the shelf *less* of a choice, which
is the one thing both readings agree is wrong.

| | `v2-004` | now |
|---|---|---|
| relics bought per run | 11.2 | **8.8** |
| gold spent | 699g | **503g** |
| gold unspent at end | 166g | **124g** |

Relics are more affordable each, and the run has less spare gold. That is the
shape §4.2's closing paragraph asks for.

## What it costs, and what it buys

| metric | `v2-004` config | this | §11.5 | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| win rate | 59.6% | **58.1%** | 20–30% | OFF, both |
| act I death rate | 8.9% | **10.9%** | 10–20% | **OFF → OK** |
| win rate, no relics | 44.5% | **33.9%** | <2% | OFF, both |
| median relics at death | 4 | 5 | 3–5 | OK, both |
| word-luck deaths | 23.0% | 21.5% | <5% | OFF, both |

**One §11.5 target moves from OFF to OK** and two others improve. The win rate
is 1.5 points harder than the baseline, so the change pays for itself.

The pricing change on its own is worth **+5.5 points** of win rate (57.0% →
62.5% with the income restored, 400 runs) and 9 points of no-relic win rate.
The income cut pays for it with a little to spare. **The no-relic figure is the
real gain**: 10.6 points off the metric `v2-004` treated as the clearest
statement of the floor.

**§11.5's band is not reached and this snapshot does not try to reach it.**
58.1% against 20–30% is `v2-004`'s open finding, unchanged in kind. What is new
is measured evidence for the lever it nominated and left for a design call.

## The design call `v2-004` left open, now measured

400 runs each, on top of this change:

| lever | win | no relics | act I deaths | relics/run |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **shipped** | **57.0%** | **33.8%** | **10.5%** | **8.8** |
| node gold back to 40/70/120 | 62.5% | 42.8% | 9.3% | 11.2 |
| node gold 25/45/80 | 56.3% | 31.0% | 10.0% | 7.9 |
| node gold 20/35/65 | 50.2% | 28.5% | 12.0% | 6.6 |
| **`payoutBase` 6 → 5** | **13.8%** | **0.8%** | 28.7% | 5.3 |

Two findings, neither applied:

1. **Income cannot reach the band.** Even 20/35/65 — half of §3.3's original —
   only reaches 50.2%, and it buys that by pushing Act I deaths to 12%.
   `v2-004` called income "the cheapest change". It is, and it is not enough.
2. **`payoutBase` 5 reaches the band and overshoots it.** 13.8% against 20–30%,
   with the no-relic target met outright at **0.8%** against <2% — the first
   configuration measured to hit that at all. The cost is Act I deaths at 28.7%
   against a 10–20% target. `v2-004` called this "the biggest single lever and
   the most dangerous"; both halves are confirmed.

The shape of an answer is probably `payoutBase` 5 with something given back to
Act I. That is a design call about what the first act should feel like, and
`v2-004` deliberately left it open. It stays open here too.

**`cfg.economy.bankrollStart` cannot be swept.** §9 gives each character a
`bankroll_start` in `relics.json` and `startRun` reads that, so the config
figure is a spec echo with nothing downstream of it — a sweep of it returns
byte-identical rows. This is not a defect (§9 is normative and the engine
follows it) but it matters for reading `v2-004`, which named the starting
bankroll "the whole difficulty curve": that lever lives in `relics.json`, not
in the harness's config override.

## `RL.22` Polyglot was inverted — R-053

Its rule is *"the shop that follows them stocks one rarity tier higher."* The
implementation drew the shelf normally and then priced each relic one tier up:

```js
const rarity = tierUp ? raiseTier(chosen.rarity) : chosen.rarity;
stock.push({ code: chosen.code, price: PRICE[rarity] ?? ... });
```

So a boss relic bought to improve your shops made them **more expensive and no
better** — the exact penalty the code comment beside it said it was avoiding. It
was invisible because there was no notion of what a shelf stocks *at*: with no
rarity weighting there was no tier to raise, so the price was the only thing
left to move.

With §6.6 the fix is what the rule always said. Tier-up shifts the act's rarity
shares up one rung. Act I, measured:

| | plain | tier-up |
|---|---|---|
| COMMON | 32.8% | **0%** |
| UNCOMMON | 56.8% | 57.4% |
| RARE | 10.3% | **42.6%** |
| shelf cost | 223g | 291g |

RARE keeps its own share as well as receiving UNCOMMON's: there is no tier above
it, and dropping it would make the relic lower the ceiling it is sold as
raising. The shelf costs more because it is better.

## Still open

1. **§11.5's band.** Unchanged from `v2-004`, and now measured — see above.
2. **The act curve is a first guess.** 35/55/10 → 28/55/17 → 20/52/28 was fitted
   to the registry's shape, not measured to an outcome. `cfg.rarityWeights` is
   sweepable like any other tunable and nobody has swept it.
3. **Three COMMON relics is not many.** The curve is bent around that scarcity
   rather than being free to choose. If §6.4's impact test is going to keep the
   registry at 3/10/5, "COMMON" names a tier with almost nothing in it, and the
   honest fix is content rather than weights.
4. **Gate 2 is a human question.** The claim is that a shelf you can act on
   beats one you cannot, and that a RARE at 10% in Act I reads as a find. A bot
   that buys the cheapest affordable thing cannot report on either.
5. **Word-luck deaths, 21.5% against <5%.** Untouched; §11.5 is explicit that
   this one is curation, not economy.
